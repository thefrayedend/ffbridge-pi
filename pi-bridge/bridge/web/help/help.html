Flash Bridge turns a small board (Orange Pi Zero 4) into a control screen for your FlashForge printer: live camera, print control, multi-colour filament matching, AI failure detection, and a connection for OrcaSlicer and Mainsail. You open it in any web browser on your phone, tablet or computer.

You need

  • The board with its SD card and a USB-C power supply (5 V / 3 A or better)
  • A FlashForge Creator 5 / 5 Pro, AD5X, or Adventurer 5M / 5M Pro on your home network

Steps

  1. Flash the SD card with the Flash Bridge image (balenaEtcher or Raspberry Pi Imager → “Use custom”).
  2. Put in your Wi-Fi details. After flashing, unplug and re-insert the card. Your computer shows a small drive called FFBRIDGE. Open ffbridge-config.txt on it and fill in two lines: 
    wifi_name=YourWiFiName
wifi_password=YourWiFiPassword
    Upper and lower case matter. Save, then eject the card. (Using a network cable? Skip this step.)
  3. Start the board. Put the card in, plug in the power and wait about 2 minutes. The first start takes longest.
  4. Open the page. On a device on the same Wi-Fi, go to http://ffbridge.local. If that doesn't open, see Wi-Fi and finding the board.
  5. Connect your printer — see Connect your printer.
Everything on this page also has a small ? next to it that jumps to the matching help topic.

Open the page

Go to http://ffbridge.local. Some Android phones and older Windows PCs don't understand “.local” names. Use the board's number address instead (for example http://192.168.1.57). You can find it in three ways:

  • Your router's app or web page lists a device called ffbridge.
  • Turn the board off, put the SD card in your computer and open MY-BRIDGE-ADDRESS.txt on the FFBRIDGE drive.
  • The same file, WIFI-STATUS.txt, shows the address once the board is online.

If the Wi-Fi doesn't connect

Turn the board off, put the SD card in your computer and open WIFI-STATUS.txt on the FFBRIDGE drive. It says in plain words what went wrong — for example “wrong password”, “can't see a network called …”, a stray space in the name, or a password that's too short — and lists the Wi-Fi networks the board can see. Fix ffbridge-config.txt and start the board again.

The setup hotspot

If the board has no network about 90 seconds after starting, it makes its own Wi-Fi network:

Network nameFlashForge-Bridge-XXXX (last 4 characters differ per board)
Passwordflashforge
Page addresshttp://10.42.0.1 (phones usually open it by themselves)

Connect your phone to it, open Settings → Wi-Fi, pick your home network and type the password. Your phone then loses the hotspot — reconnect it to your home Wi-Fi and open http://ffbridge.local. If the hotspot comes back after about a minute, the details were wrong: connect to it again and the Wi-Fi tab shows why.

Every 5 minutes the board tries your saved Wi-Fi again (in case the router was off), but never while a phone is connected to the hotspot.

A network cable to your router always works and needs no setup.

1. Turn on LAN-only mode on the printer

On the printer's touch screen open Settings → Network (Wi-Fi or Ethernet) and turn on LAN-only mode. The printer now shows an 8-character pairing code (also called check code). Keep that screen open.

2. Find the printer

Open Settings (gear) → Advanced → Printer connection and press Scan network. Your printer appears under “Found on your network”. Press it.

3. Enter the pairing code

Type the code from the printer screen, press Test connection and then Save. The printer screen of the bridge opens.

Good to know

  • More options in this window: serial number (found by the scan), the port for OrcaSlicer/Mainsail, a different camera address, “Level the bed before each print”, and “Clear the platform automatically”.
  • More than one printer? See Several printers.
  • Give the printer a fixed address in your router (often called “DHCP reservation”) so it keeps the same IP.

One board can run several printers. Add each one in Settings → Advanced → Printer connection.

On this screen

Each printer gets a tab at the top showing its name and status — tap one to switch. On a phone, the tabs become a drop-down list at the top instead.

In OrcaSlicer and Mainsail

Each printer gets its own port: the first uses 7125, the second 7126, and so on. In OrcaSlicer, set up one printer per machine, each with its own address (for example ffbridge.local:7125 and ffbridge.local:7126). The slicer then sends each file to the right printer by itself. The addresses are listed in Settings → Advanced → Mainsail & OrcaSlicer.

  1. Press Print a file on the printer screen.
  2. Pick a file from the list, or press Upload a sliced file (or drag one onto it) to add a .gcode / .gcode.3mf from your computer or phone.
  3. Check the filaments (multi-colour printers), tick Level the bed before printing if you want, and press Start print.

Files have to be sliced — in OrcaSlicer, FlashPrint, Orca-FlashForge, or with the bridge's own slicer.

Delete removes a file from the board only. Files stored on the printer itself can only be deleted on the printer's screen.

On the AD5X and Creator 5, each filament in the slicer file has to be matched to one of the 4 slots. The bridge does this the way the printer does:

  • It prefers a slot with the same material (PLA, PETG, …), then the closest colour.
  • Each row shows a verdict: Match, Close colour, Different colour or Different material!
  • Tap a slot button in a row to change it.

Prints sent from OrcaSlicer

If everything matches, the print starts straight away. If something doesn't match, the print waits and this window pops up on the printer screen (and in Mainsail):

Always ask?

In Settings → General → Multi-colour prints you can choose “Always ask me before a multi-colour print starts”.

Keep the slot colours right (tap a slot on the printer screen) — matching is only as good as what the printer thinks is loaded.

OrcaSlicer can send prints to the bridge as if it were a Klipper printer.

  1. In OrcaSlicer, select your printer and click the Wi-Fi / connection icon next to it (“Physical printer” settings).
  2. Host type: Octo/Klipper
  3. Hostname, IP or URL: the address shown in Settings → Advanced → Mainsail & OrcaSlicer, for example 192.168.1.57:7125. Press Copy there and paste it.
  4. Leave the API key empty and press Test. Then OK.
  • After slicing, use Print to send and start, or Send to only upload (start it later from Print a file).
  • Multi-colour prints that need your OK pop up on the printer screen — see Multi-colour prints.
  • OrcaSlicer's Device tab shows Mainsail for this printer.
  • With a second printer, use its own port (7126, …).

After every print, FlashForge printers show a “print finished” message and refuse to start the next print until the plate is confirmed clear.

Auto clear platform

Turn on Auto clear platform under Controls (or in the printer's settings) and the bridge clears the message by itself about 8 seconds after a print finishes or is cancelled. It does not clear after a printer error, so you still see what went wrong.

With auto clear on, a print started from your phone or OrcaSlicer could begin on a plate that still has a part on it. Take finished prints off promptly.

Starting a print from the bridge also clears a finished job first, so you only need the button when starting from the printer's own screen.

On the AD5X and Creator 5 the Filament card shows the 4 slots of the material station. A blue frame marks the slot in use while printing.

  • The colours are the printer's own colour list — the printer only accepts these.
  • AD5X: the slot window also has Load filament and Unload filament.
  • Adventurer 5M / 5M Pro have no material station, so this card is hidden.

Temperatures

Tap a temperature to set it. Quick buttons fill in typical values; Off turns the heater off.

Orange numbers are still heating up. The Creator 5 shows each of its 4 nozzles; the chamber only appears on printers that report it.

Moving the axes and editing printer.cfg are not possible: FlashForge printers don't allow that over the network.

While a print runs, the board looks at a camera picture every 10 seconds with Obico's open-source AI model, which recognises “spaghetti” and similar failures. Everything runs on the board — no pictures leave your network.

Settings

  • On / off for everything (top switch) and per printer (switch on each printer's card).
  • Alert me — shows a red bar on the printer screen, a pop-up in Mainsail and (if set up) a Discord message with a picture.
  • Alert me and pause the print — also pauses. Pausing needs a stronger signal than an alert, so you always get the warning first.
  • Sensitivity — High catches problems sooner but gives more false alarms.

Good to know

  • The first few minutes of each print are a warm-up: the AI learns what “normal” looks like before it can alert.
  • Not a failure (red bar or AI page) mutes alerts for the rest of that print.
  • Test now analyses one picture and shows what the AI saw, with boxes around suspicious areas.
  • Good lighting helps: turn the printer light on.

Discord notifications

  1. In Discord, open your server's Server Settings → Integrations → Webhooks → New Webhook.
  2. Pick the channel, then Copy Webhook URL.
  3. Paste it into Discord webhook URL, choose what you want to hear about and press Save. Send test message checks it.

“Progress update every N minutes” sends a progress message (with a camera picture if ticked) while printing; 0 = only events.

Spoolman

If you run a Spoolman server, enter its address. Filament use is taken off the active spool when a print ends.

Each printer also has a full Mainsail page (the well-known Klipper web interface), on its own port: http://ffbridge.local:7125 for the first printer. Links are in Settings → Advanced → Mainsail & OrcaSlicer.

Mainsail runs separately from this screen and is mainly there because OrcaSlicer's Device tab shows it. Because FlashForge printers aren't Klipper printers, many of its panels do nothing (moving axes, macros, printer.cfg). Everything that works is also on this screen.

  • Password for this page (Advanced → System): optional. Without it, anyone on your home network can open this page. Any user name works with the password.
  • Restart / Shut down the board. Running prints are not affected. After shutting down, unplug and re-plug the power to start it again.
  • Log: what the bridge did recently — useful when something doesn't work.
  • Board name (Wi-Fi tab): with more than one bridge, rename one (e.g. ffbridge2) so each has its own address.

Other settings in ffbridge-config.txt

printer_ip=              add a printer without the setup page …
printer_code=            … with its pairing code
printer_name=
timezone=                e.g. America/New_York
ssh=no                   advanced: remote login as user "ffadmin"
ssh_password=            at least 6 characters

Changes in the file are applied the next time the board starts.

The board checks GitHub for a new version twice a day. When there is one, a dot appears on the ⚙ gear.

  1. Open Advanced → System → Updates. It shows what's new.
  2. Press Install update. It takes 1–2 minutes; the page reloads by itself.
  • Your printers and settings are kept.
  • Wait until no print is being sent from this page. A print already running on the printer isn't affected.
  • If the new version doesn't start, the board puts the old version back by itself and tells you.
  • Rarely an update needs a new SD card image (for example a new AI model). The page then says so: download your backup, flash the new image from the link shown, and the settings come back by themselves.

A backup is one small file with your printers (including pairing codes), AI, Discord and Spoolman settings, the page password, print history and Mainsail settings. Print files are not included.

  • Download backup (Advanced → System) saves it to your phone or computer.
  • Restore: choose a backup file. The bridge restarts with those settings.
  • Automatic copy: the board keeps an up-to-date ffbridge-backup.json on the SD card's FFBRIDGE drive.

Moving to a new SD card

  1. Put the old card in your computer and copy ffbridge-backup.json from the FFBRIDGE drive.
  2. Flash the new card, take it out and put it back in, and paste the file onto its FFBRIDGE drive (next to ffbridge-config.txt).
  3. Start the board. Your printers and settings are restored by themselves.

The backup contains your printers' pairing codes, so keep it private.

When the printer reports an error, a red box appears on the printer screen with the error code, what it means in plain English and what to try. Repair guide ↗ opens FlashForge's page for your printer model.

The same message goes to Discord if you use it.

Flash Bridge can slice for you, right in the browser: put models on the plate, paint colours, check every layer, then print — no OrcaSlicer on your PC needed. It uses the real OrcaSlicer engine running on the board.

Switching it on

Settings → General → Slicer → Download the slicer engine (once, about 35 MB; it needs the board to have internet). The switch on that card shows or hides Prepare · Paint · Preview in the top bar.

Your printer's profiles only

The slicer always uses the Flashforge profiles of the printer you have open (for example Flashforge Creator 5 Pro 0.4 nozzle). Opening a project made for another printer — say a MakerWorld file for a Bambu P2S — keeps its models, colours and painting but never switches the printer or its settings. The board checks this too, so nothing else can slip in. Only the nozzle size, plate type, quality profile and Flashforge filament profiles of your printer can be chosen.

Prepare

  • Add STL, OBJ or 3MF files with the + button or by dropping them on the 3D view. Modifier / helper parts in a project are left out.
  • Click a model to select it, then Move (M), Rotate (R) or Scale (S) with the handles, or type exact numbers in the Objects card. Models always sit on the plate.
  • Lay on face (F): click the side that should be on the plate. Auto-orient puts the biggest flat side down. Arrange spreads everything out on the plate.
  • Filaments: the colours of your project — up to 16, more than the printer has heads (+ Add filament, × removes one). A project brings its own colours. Use loaded gives the first ones the spools loaded in the printer. Each object has its own filament (the number next to its name).
  • Each plate is sliced with only the filaments it uses, so one plate can use four colours and the next four others — you swap spools between plates. If a plate uses more colours than the printer can hold at once, the slicer asks which filament prints each extra colour (the closest colour is suggested; your painting is not changed). Cancel to repaint instead.
  • Quality: the profile plus the most-used settings. All settings has everything else (search for "ironing", "seam", "speed" …). Changed settings are blue; ↺ puts one back.

Plates

Like OrcaSlicer, you can have several plates side by side. The tabs at the top of the 3D view switch plates; + Plate adds one (Page Up / Page Down also switch). Drag a model with the mouse or a finger to move it on the plate or onto another plate — dragging it past the last plate makes a new one. A model belongs to the plate it stands on; Move to in the Plate card works too. Arrange packs the current plate; what does not fit goes onto the next plate. Projects with several plates (MakerWorld, Bambu, Orca) open with all their plates, each centred on one of yours; models that sat beside the plates in the project get a plate of their own. The project's support, infill, wall and brim settings come along (shown in a message; change them under Quality → All settings).

Every plate is sliced and printed on its own: in Preview the tabs switch between plates (a plate is sliced when you open it), Slice all plates does them one after another, and Print plate N… prints the one you are looking at. A green dot on a tab means its slice is up to date.

Paint

Three things can be painted, as in OrcaSlicer: Colour (which filament), Supports (blue = support here, red = never support here) and Seam (blue = put the seam here, red = never here).

  • Circle: paints what you see inside the circle. Sphere: everything inside the ball, also around corners.
  • Triangle: whole triangles of the model. Height range: a horizontal band (stripes, a colour change at a height).
  • Fill: click to fill the connected area that has the same paint; with edge detection it stops at edges sharper than the angle (one face of a box, a flat top).
  • Gap fill: fills small unpainted spots left between painted areas with the paint around them.
  • Section view cuts the model away from the top so you can reach inside surfaces.
  • Shift + drag erases; Alt + wheel changes the brush size; Ctrl + wheel moves the section; 1–9 pick a filament; Ctrl+Z undoes.
  • Painted supports only print when Supports is switched on. The Supports tab offers a button for that ("only where painted"), can highlight overhangs and can put enforcers on all of them.

Painting from MakerWorld / Bambu / Orca projects (colours, supports and seams) is kept.

Slice & Preview

Preview slices the current plate on the board (a few seconds to a few minutes on the Orange Pi) and shows it layer by layer: drag the slider on the right or use the arrow keys. Line type colours walls, infill, supports … and lets you hide types by clicking them. The side panel shows print time and filament used per head.

Print… opens the normal print window with the filament check, exactly like a file from OrcaSlicer. G-code / 3MF save the sliced file.

Good to know

  • The models live in this browser tab; reloading the page empties the plates (the last slices stay on the board). Save project (Plate card) downloads everything — all plates, positions, filaments and painting — as a 3MF you can open here again with Add model, or in OrcaSlicer / Bambu Studio.
  • Keys in Prepare: M / R / S / F tools, arrow keys nudge the selected model 1 mm (Shift: 10 mm), Ctrl+D duplicates, X mirrors left-right (painting included), Del deletes, Ctrl+Z undoes, Page Up / Down switch plates. The size fields (Width / Depth / Height mm) scale the model evenly.
  • Very detailed models (more than 1.5 million triangles, e.g. scans and sculpts) are simplified when opened to about 1 million: the shape moves at most 0.05 mm and the painting stays exactly as it was — still far finer than the nozzle can print. Very big models are slow on phones; a PC works best for painting.
  • Infill filament (Quality card): prints the inside of every model with one filament, for example cheap white, also on multi-colour prints. The outside keeps its colours as a skin about 3 mm thick; top and bottom surfaces and walls are never printed with it. The infill filament counts as one of the printer's colours on each plate.
  • If the models leave no room for the prime tower (the purge block for colour changes), a printer with one nozzle per colour such as the Creator 5 prints without it.
  • While the slicer works, AI failure detection is paused so the slicer gets the board's memory and processor. It starts again by itself about 20 seconds after the last slice. A print that is running is not watched during that time.
  • The slicer uses at most 3 processor cores, so the printer screens and web pages stay quick. If a plate is too big for the board's memory, the slice stops with a message (the bridge keeps running): put fewer models on that plate or use a larger layer height.
  • The engine is the OrcaSlicer engine packaged by Helio Additive (slicer-cli), AGPL-3.0. The source link is under the slice button.

Settings → General → Screen layout changes how the printer screen is arranged:

  • Camera size: how much of the width the camera gets (50–85%). The side panel gets the rest, but never less than 300 pixels.
  • Side panel: right, left, or always below the camera.
  • Print status card (Print a file, Clear platform, progress, Pause/Stop): under the camera, or at the top of the side panel so it is always in view.
  • Keep the whole camera picture on screen: on short or wide windows the camera gets smaller instead of making you scroll.

The settings are saved in the browser you set them in, so your phone and your PC can each have their own. Default puts everything back. On phones everything is always in one column.

An optional advanced mode. The board's Wi-Fi becomes a private network just for your printers, and the board connects to your home network with a network cable. The printers can't reach the internet and nothing on the internet can reach them. You keep using this page, Mainsail and OrcaSlicer exactly as before, because they all go through the board.

Switching it on

  1. Plug a network cable from your router into the board. Open this page and check Settings → Wi-Fi shows Ethernet: Connected.
  2. Settings → Advanced → Printer network: choose a name and password for the printer Wi-Fi, press Switch on.
  3. On each printer's screen, connect its Wi-Fi to that network. The bridge finds each printer at its new address by itself within about 2 minutes; the page lists the printers that joined.

Internet for the printers

Blocked by default. For a printer firmware update, press Allow for 1 hour (or 4 hours, or until you turn it off). It switches itself off when the time is up; Block now ends it early.

Good to know

  • FlashPrint, the FlashForge app and OrcaSlicer's own FlashForge connection can't reach the printers directly. Use OrcaSlicer's Klipper/Octo connection to the board (port 7125…) instead — see Sending from OrcaSlicer.
  • The board's Wi-Fi antenna is small: keep the printers reasonably close to it.
  • The board gives the printers the time, so their clocks stay right without internet.
  • If you can't reach the page over the cable any more, connect a phone or laptop to the printer Wi-Fi and open http://10.43.0.1.
  • Switch off puts the board back on your home Wi-Fi (if one is saved). Then connect the printers to your home Wi-Fi again on their screens.
