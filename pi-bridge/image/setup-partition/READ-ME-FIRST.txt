FlashForge Bridge - quick start
===============================

What you need
-------------
* This SD card (already prepared - you're reading it!)
* The Orange Pi Zero 4 board and a USB-C power supply (5V / 3A or better)
* Your FlashForge printer (Creator 5 / 5 Pro, AD5X, Adventurer 5M / 5M Pro)
  on the same home network

Step 1 - Wi-Fi  (skip if you plug the board into your router with a cable)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Open  ffbridge-config.txt  on this drive (double-click it, it opens in Notepad).
Fill in:
    wifi_name=YourWiFiName
    wifi_password=YourWiFiPassword
Save the file. Eject the SD card safely.

Step 2 - Start the board
------------------------
Put the SD card into the board and plug in the power.
The first start takes about 2 minutes (it sets itself up). Later starts are faster.

Step 3 - Open the setup page
----------------------------
On a phone, tablet or computer on the same Wi-Fi, open:

    http://ffbridge.local

Doesn't open?
 * Wait one more minute and try again.
 * Look in your router's app for a device called "ffbridge" and open its address,
   e.g. http://192.168.1.57
 * Put the SD card back in your computer: the file MY-BRIDGE-ADDRESS.txt on it
   shows the board's address (it appears once the board was online).
 * Wi-Fi details wrong? The board then makes its own Wi-Fi network called
   "FlashForge-Bridge-XXXX" (password: flashforge). Connect your phone to it -
   the setup page opens by itself (or go to http://10.42.0.1) - and pick your
   home Wi-Fi there.

Step 4 - Add your printer
-------------------------
On the printer's screen: Settings > Network > turn on "LAN-only mode".
It shows a pairing code. On the setup page press "Scan network", pick your
printer, type the pairing code, press "Test connection", then "Save".

Done! Open Mainsail from the setup page, or connect OrcaSlicer
(host type "Octo/Klipper", address shown on the setup page).

AI failure detection
--------------------
Switched on by default. While a print runs, the board looks at the printer's
camera and warns you about "spaghetti" failures. On the "AI Watch" page you can
choose to also pause the print automatically.
